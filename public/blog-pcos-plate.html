The PCOS plate, without counting anything

Three things on every main meal, one rule about tea, and why we stopped talking about calories.

[[Dietitian name], RD
4 November 2026 · 6 min read
In this postThree things on the plateThe tea ruleWhy no caloriesDinner cutoff

Most PCOS advice arrives as a spreadsheet. Grams of this, a ceiling on that. It is accurate and almost nobody follows it past week two, because a spreadsheet does not help you at 1 PM with guests at the door.

Here is the version my clients actually keep.

Three things on the plate

Every main meal has a protein source, a fibre source and a fat you can name. Eggs, daal or chicken; a vegetable or a salad that is more than a garnish; ghee, nuts or oil you can see. If those three are there, the plate is doing its job for insulin, whatever the portion.

The tea rule

Iron and calcium supplements are wasted if tea arrives within half an hour. Milk tea twice a day is fine; it is the timing that matters. This is the one rule I ask apps to enforce, and the one Saathi builds into reminder spacing automatically.

Why no calories

Calorie counting works in trials because somebody weighs the food. At home it becomes a guess that makes you feel worse about a guess. Structure (what is on the plate, when) is something you can check with your eyes.

Dinner cutoff

Two hours before sleep. Not 8 PM for everyone; two hours before your sleep. If you sleep at 11:30, dinner by 9:30 is the target, and a late day is not a failed day.
