Check my diet plan

Paste the plan you were given — by a dietitian, a doctor, or yourself — and get the same structural check that runs inside Saathi: gaps, protein at each main meal, tea away from iron, dinner before sleep, water. No calories, no food database, no sign-up. It runs in your browser and nothing is sent anywhere.

Your day

A structural check only. It is not medical advice, and it cannot tell whether the amounts are right for you — a dietitian does that.

And then the hard part

A checked plan still has to happen at 1 p.m. on a Tuesday.

That's the whole reason Saathi exists. The same plan goes into the app and becomes alarms that ring until you answer, swaps from your own kitchen when guests arrive, a walk after each main meal, and a one-minute recap on Sunday.

What exactly is checked?
  • The longest gap between eating moments, flagged over five hours
  • A protein source at every main meal
  • Dinner at least two hours before you sleep
  • Tea or coffee kept 30 minutes away from iron or calcium
  • Refined and sugary items, with a swap offered
  • Water across the day, six cups minimum
Why no calories?

Because counting them is the part people stop doing. Saathi checks the shape of a plan instead, and the only calorie figure anywhere in the app is an optional daily target for a weight-loss goal.

Is my plan stored?

No. The check runs entirely in your browser. Nothing is uploaded, nothing is logged, and closing the tab is the end of it.

It flagged something my dietitian wrote.

Then your dietitian wins. This is a general structural check; a professional writing for your specific situation may have a reason. The same is true inside the app: a dietitian's plan is never overruled by the checker.
